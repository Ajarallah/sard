# Track 6: Arabic explanatory tradition and domain-specific explanation

## Findings
**The classical شرح is a layered explanation, and each layer has a different job.** The متن condenses, the شرح opens up, the حاشية resolves remaining difficulties and adds qualifications, and the تقرير records what came up in teaching [6.1][6.4]. The working method of a شارح is فك العبارة, then تصوير المسألة, then الاستدلال, scaled to the learner's level [6.2]. sard's foundation, stations and arrival already reproduce this arc. What sard lacks are the حاشية's two signature moves: flagging قيود المسائل, the exceptions and limits that live elsewhere [6.3], and labelled تنبيهات [6.17].

**الفنقلة is the native Arabic way to anticipate the reader's objection.** «فإن قيل… قلنا» exists "لتقرير معنى، أو بيان دليله، أو دفع ما يُشكل عليه" [6.5]. Modern research names the same need: experts cannot see what novices will stumble on [6.37], and learners carry predictable misconceptions, for example about code [6.24]. sard has «لكنّ» as a connector but no rule that one station should take on the most likely misunderstanding.

**Arabic rhetoric gives sard's connector rule a deeper basis than "be logical."** al-Jurjani's tradition treats the choice to link or not link as the core of eloquence ("معرفة الفصل من الوصل"). A و-link implies a reason the listener will look for, ف implies immediacy and ثم implies a gap [6.7][6.8]. sard currently lists ثم under neutral sequence, which is slightly wrong in Arabic. The tradition also recognizes كمال الاتصال: when the next sentence explains or restates the previous one, the most correct link is often an explanatory «أي»/«بعبارة أخرى», or no conjunction at all. A mechanical «ثم» or «ولهذا» there is a defect.

**Cohesion is more than connectors.** Text linguistics separates سبك (surface links) from حبك (semantic coherence) [6.9], and Halliday and Hasan list five cohesion devices, of which conjunction is only one [6.10]. A sard output can have a bold connector on every paragraph and still fail if terms drift or the relations do not hold. The pre-send check should test the حبك ("can I state the relation in one clause?") and term consistency, not just whether connectors are present.

**The tradition already separates what the text says from what the explainer infers.** تفسير is meaning from the wording and تأويل is meaning by إشارة [6.11]. This is exactly sard's split between the author's words and «لفتة من عندي», and it gives the owner a classical vocabulary for that rule. al-Jahiz's definition of بيان judges an explanation by whether it uncovers the meaning, not by its form [6.12]. That is permission for a figure or worked example when it serves the narrative. The word سرد itself means تتابع: things brought in sequence [6.13].

**Ausubel splits the "foundation" into two kinds.** An expository organizer (definitions) fits a wholly new topic, and a comparative organizer (likeness and difference to what the reader knows) fits a partly known one [6.14]. 3Blue1Brown adds that in maths, definitions are "an ending point", so a concrete case should come first [6.21]. Together with the expertise reversal effect [6.36], this means the foundation should vary by reader and domain rather than always being a glossary paragraph.

**Mathematics and code both need a worked example and a mental model inside the narrative.** Worked examples reduce load for novices [6.18]. Good math explanation pairs intuition with rigour and names the misleading intuition [6.19][6.20]. Developers spend most of their time understanding code and mostly lack the rationale behind it [6.22][6.23], and confusion comes from missing mental models and transferred misconceptions [6.24]. A code station sequence that fits sard's spine is: what it does, then the model of how it runs, a trace through one input, and why it was built this way.

**Law and medicine each come with their own causal spine.** Law runs Issue, then Rule, then Application, then Conclusion, and ICRA's conclusion-first form matches sard's compass [6.26]. The key honesty rule in law is holding versus dicta [6.25]. Medicine runs enabling conditions, then fault, then consequences, taught by compare-and-contrast and thinking aloud, and connected scripts measurably beat lists [6.27][6.28]. Both map onto sard stations without bullets.

**History and literature push sard's rules from two directions.** History needs multiple causes, evidence and perspectives [6.29]. A single clean causal chain can be a falsely honest «ولهذا», so the stations must admit «ومن جهة أخرى» when causation is contested. Literature, scripture and legal clauses need close reading: word choice, syntax and the order of the passage itself [6.30]. There, "logic order over page order" should yield to the text's own order, which is the classical line-by-line متن/شرح layout.

**Visual and interactive work strengthens narrative only when the narrative stays the guide.** Dual coding [6.35] and the Arabic mind-map study [6.15] support one well-chosen figure. 3Blue1Brown insists the visual must say the same thing as the narration [6.21]. Tufte's critique of PowerPoint shows how bullet hierarchies hide reasoning [6.31]. Bret Victor and Nicky Case show that interaction works when the author "guides the reader" [6.32][6.34]. The lesson for sard is that a visual can accompany a station but never replace it.

**Explanation must fit the audience and be testable.** The curse of knowledge cannot be removed by awareness alone [6.37], so the skill must force the explainer to list what the reader needs first. Plain-language practice is to name the audience and test for understanding [6.38], and Arabic pedagogy likewise links the new lesson to the old [6.16]. The classical aim was الملكة, the ability to do something, not recall [6.6].

## Gaps in sard today
- No objection-and-answer move (الفنقلة): the most likely misunderstanding is never named and answered on purpose.
- No slot for limits and exceptions (قيود المسائل / تنبيه): the arrival states the meaning but not where it stops applying.
- The foundation has a single mode. sard does not distinguish a new topic (expository) from a partly known one (comparative), and does not let maths start from a concrete case.
- No audience setting. Novice, informed and expert readers get the same foundation length and the same terminology density.
- The connector list treats ثم as neutral sequence, has no explanatory «أي/بعبارة أخرى» for restating stations, and the checks test only that connectors exist, not حبك or term consistency.
- No domain guidance beyond "use the field's logic". There is no spine for maths (worked example, intuition then rigour), code (behaviour, model, trace, rationale), law (IRAC, holding vs dicta), medicine (conditions, fault, consequences, compare), history (multi-causal, perspectives, evidence), literature (close reading) or charts (encoding, pattern, exception, what the chart doesn't show).
- "Follow the idea's logic, not page order" has no exception for texts whose wording and order are the point: poetry, scripture, statutes, a single function.
- No guidance on when one figure would uncover the meaning better than a paragraph (dual coding, بيان), and no rule that the figure must restate the narration.
- The closing line offers a next chapter, but there is no optional check-for-understanding or "try it" for procedural domains.

## Proposals for sard
- P6.1: Add a «إيراد وجواب» station: at most one per explanation, opened with «فإن قيل…» or «قد يُظن أنّ… لكنّ…», which names the most likely misunderstanding and answers it from the source. (sources: 6.5, 6.37, 6.24) It fits the narrative core because it is itself a connector-led station in the classical شرح voice, not a list.
- P6.2: Give the foundation two modes. Use an expository foundation for new topics (terms and context) and a comparative foundation when the reader partly knows the topic («يشبه ما تعرفه من… ويختلف في…»). In maths or code, the foundation may be one concrete case instead of a definition. (sources: 6.14, 6.21, 6.36) This keeps "start from the foundation" but makes the foundation fit the reader.
- P6.3: Rewrite the connector table by meaning, based on الفصل والوصل: و for addition, ف for immediate consequence, ثم for a step after a gap, لكنّ for correction, and «أي/بعبارة أخرى» for explanatory restatement (كمال الاتصال). Add a pre-send check: "For each connector, can I state the relation in one clause?" Also check that key terms stay the same throughout (حبك and lexical cohesion). (sources: 6.7, 6.8, 6.9, 6.10) This deepens the existing "connectors must be true" rule rather than adding a new mechanism.
- P6.4: Add a reference file of domain lenses (`references/domains.md`). Each lens gives the natural station spine, written as prose cues rather than headings:
  - law: facts, issue, rule, application, holding (dicta flagged)
  - medicine: enabling conditions, fault, consequences, compared with a look-alike
  - maths: concrete case, intuition, formal step, why it holds, the misleading intuition
  - code: what it does, mental model, one traced input, why it is built this way
  - history: context, multiple causes, event, consequences, other perspectives, evidence
  - literature: word, image, structure, meaning
  - charts: what is measured, how it is encoded, the pattern, the exception, what the chart does not show

  (sources: 6.18, 6.19, 6.22, 6.24, 6.25, 6.26, 6.27, 6.29, 6.30, 6.31) The lens only chooses the order of stations, and the output stays connected prose.
- P6.5: Add an audience setting that is inferred from the request and asked about only if unclear. A novice gets a fuller foundation, a worked example and terms defined on first use. An informed reader gets a comparative foundation. An expert gets a one-sentence foundation with no definitions, and the stations carry the argument. (sources: 6.36, 6.37, 6.38, 6.2) The شارح tradition itself scaled the شرح to the student's level [6.2].
- P6.6: Add an optional «تنبيه» line after the arrival: one or two sentences on the source's own limits, exceptions or conditions (قيود المسائل, holding vs dicta, the intuition rigour corrects). Keep it separate from «لفتة من عندي», which is the explainer's inference. (sources: 6.3, 6.17, 6.25, 6.19) This is a native شرح form that sharpens the arrival without breaking the prose.
- P6.7: Add a text-anchored mode, either a `/sard-sharh` command or a mode of sard, for short dense texts where wording and order matter (a poem, verse, legal clause or function). Each station quotes a fragment of the متن and then explains it (فك العبارة, then meaning, then why it matters), keeping the text's order. It still ends with an arrival and «الرسالة باختصار». (sources: 6.1, 6.2, 6.30, 6.4) It applies the same narrative rules on top of the classical متن/شرح layout.
- P6.8: Name the تفسير/تأويل split in the rules. Rule 4 becomes: the author's words in quotes, «ما يقوله النص» (تفسير, from the wording), and «لفتة من عندي» (تأويل, reached by إشارة), with uncertainty stated in the لفتة. (sources: 6.11, 6.12) This gives sard's most distinctive rule its classical name, which helps Arabic users and the owner's framing.
- P6.9: Add an optional visual-companion rule. When the material is spatial, quantitative, procedural or comparative, one figure (diagram, small table or mind map) may sit beside one station, and it must restate that station's point, not add new claims. Interaction (one adjustable parameter) is allowed only in sard-deck or artifact output. (sources: 6.35, 6.15, 6.21, 6.31, 6.32) In al-Jahiz's terms the figure serves بيان, and the narrative stays the guide.
- P6.10: Allow the final line to be a check instead of a next step. For procedural domains (maths, code, law problems), it can be one self-test question or a "try it on this input" prompt, while still offering only one next step. (sources: 6.6, 6.18, 6.38) Classical teaching aimed at الملكة, and checking understanding completes the arrival rather than turning it into a list.

## Cautions
- **Domain lenses must not become templates with headings.** IRAC headings, SOAP sections or "Facts / Issue / Rule" labels would bring the bullet structure back. Each lens only orders the stations, the connectors still carry the logic, and the output stays prose [6.31].
- **Use الفنقلة sparingly.** The classical شروح overused «فإن قيل» until texts became dialectical and long. Allow one, at most two, and only for a misunderstanding the reader is likely to have. Never use straw objections.
- **Copy the functions of the tradition, not its layering.** متن, then شرح, then حاشية, then تقرير produced famous verbosity [6.1]. sard should take فك العبارة, تصوير and الاستدلال, plus تنبيه, without stacking commentary on commentary or growing past the length rule.
- **Do not force a connector where فصل is more correct.** Arabic rhetoric warns against links without a real relation [6.7]. If every paragraph must open with a bold connector, allow «أي/بعبارة أخرى» or a true restatement rather than a fake «ولهذا».
- **Do not let the foundation turn into a glossary.** "Never start with definitions" [6.21] and the expertise reversal effect [6.36] both warn that an over-long foundation loses the reader before the first station.
- **Visuals must stay subordinate.** A figure that adds claims, or a deck that splits reasoning into bullets, defeats the core [6.31]. Interactive explorables are costly and belong only to deck or artifact outputs, never the default chat answer.
- **A single causal line can be dishonest.** In history and contested science, «ولهذا» across a multi-causal event is a false connector. The lens must allow «ومن جهة أخرى» and say plainly when causation is disputed [6.29].
- **Explanation is not advice.** For medical and legal sources, sard explains the text's reasoning. It must not turn the arrival into a diagnosis or legal opinion, and must keep the holding apart from the explainer's inference [6.25].
- **The audience setting should be inferred, not interrogated.** Asking the user several calibration questions before every explanation breaks the "answer first" default. Infer the level from the request and ask only when it is truly ambiguous.
