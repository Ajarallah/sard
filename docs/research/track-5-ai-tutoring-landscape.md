## Findings

Every major lab has converged on the same answer to "AI makes learning shallow": a mode layered on a general model that withholds or delays answers, asks questions, chunks content, and checks understanding (OpenAI study mode, Claude Learning mode, Gemini Guided Learning, Khanmigo [5.1][5.5][5.6][5.9][5.10]). Study mode in particular is only system instructions [5.5][5.6]. A skill can therefore carry real pedagogy, but LearnLM's authors warn that nuanced pedagogy resists prompting and needs few, high-leverage rules [5.8].

The causal evidence is consistent: the harm comes from substitution, not from AI itself. Unguarded GPT-4 raised practice grades and lowered exam grades, while a hint-based tutor removed most of that harm [5.15]. A tightly designed tutor that revealed one step at a time from pre-supplied correct solutions doubled learning gains against strong active learning [5.17][5.18]. Both results come from problem-solving practice, which is not sard's main use (understanding a text someone already has to read). The transferable lesson is about where the reader's effort goes, not "never explain".

The most important finding for sard concerns fluency. People who got explanations from ChatGPT overestimated their own understanding more than people who read identical text, and then explained the topic worse [5.19]. Bastani's students were "overly optimistic" about their learning [5.16], and knowledge workers who trust GenAI think less critically [5.20]. sard's whole value is producing smooth, connected, satisfying explanations, so it is exactly the kind of output most likely to create an illusion of understanding. The cheapest counter in the literature is asking the reader to explain or apply the idea themselves [5.30], or a short knowledge check [5.5].

The evidence does not support turning sard into a Socratic tutor by default. In a direct comparison, a Narrative LLM was preferred for actionable guidance and by experienced, AI-cautious users, while Socratic suited novices and AI-optimists [5.25]. Students co-designing tutors asked to toggle between Socratic and direct styles [5.28]. The right design is a narrative default with an opt-in interactive mode. When Socratic behavior is wanted, it has to be enforced explicitly, because models leak the key terms in their first reply more than 96% of the time regardless of size [5.27].

Faithfulness risk is concentrated in the parts of the output that carry sard's signature. LLM summaries systematically overgeneralize: quantifiers drop, tense moves from past to present, findings become recommendations. Telling the model to "be accurate" doubled the effect [5.23]. Hallucinations in long outputs cluster at the end [5.24], which is where sard puts the arrival, the "message in brief" and the "note of my own". Source-grounded tools like NotebookLM still hallucinate under ambiguity [5.12]. The research tools that hold up best show the exact supporting quote or location [5.13].

Sycophancy is a general property of RLHF assistants [5.22]. It matters for sard in two places: when a user says "so it means X?" and X is wrong, and in the "note of my own", which can drift toward what the user seems to want. sard has no rule for either.

Research-reading tools also show what goes wrong when explaining evidence. Consensus vote-counts studies and reads paywalled papers from abstracts only [5.14], and answer engines mix source types without labels [5.38]. sard-article (critique) and sard-roots/sard-book (external research) will make claims about fields and literatures, so they need light evidence hygiene: study type, scope, whether a claim is contested, and what kind of source it came from.

Learner modeling and ordering is where sard is already strong in spirit but informal. Every tutor prompt starts by learning the user's goal, level and prior knowledge [5.6][5.30]. The best recent tutoring result comes from making the prerequisite structure explicit and deciding what to teach next, which beat larger models [5.26]. sard's "foundation" and "order by the idea's logic, not page order" are an informal version of that prerequisite graph; making the prerequisite step explicit, and sizing the foundation to the reader, is a natural upgrade.

On product surface, NotebookLM, Guided Learning and Fabric produce many artifacts: study guide, FAQ, timeline, mind map, quiz, five-sentence summary, concept map [5.9][5.11][5.35][5.36]. Almost all of them are list-shaped, and none tells the reader why one point leads to the next. That gap is sard's niche, and the curated skill lists confirm that explanation skills are rare [5.34]. Borrowing should therefore be selective: artifacts derived from the narrative (a station map, a glossary appendix, sard-deck) rather than replacements for it.

On skill engineering, Anthropic's guidance favors short SKILL.md files with progressive disclosure into `references/`, third-person "what + when" descriptions containing trigger terms, references one level deep, scripts for deterministic work, and evaluation-driven iteration across models [5.31][5.32][5.33]. sard's files are already short (39–93 lines), which is good. It has no `references/`, no evals and no persona testing [5.10]. Its core description is one English sentence without the Arabic trigger phrases the body lists, which weakens discovery. Plugins can also ship an output style for users who want narration on every reply [5.4].

## Gaps in sard today
(Based on reading `plugins/sard/skills/*/SKILL.md`.)
- No calibration to the reader: no inference of level or prior knowledge, and no sizing of the foundation to the reader. Every reader gets the same depth.
- No check for understanding: the explanation ends with "message in brief" plus a next-step offer, with nothing that asks the reader to reconstruct or apply it. This leaves the fluency illusion [5.19] unaddressed.
- No source anchors: stations are not tied to page, slide, section or timestamp. Quotes are required, but locations are not.
- No guard on scope or hedges: nothing stops the arrival or the message from dropping quantifiers, shifting tense, or turning a finding into advice [5.23]. "If unsure, verify or say so" covers context facts, not the meaning statements.
- No final faithfulness pass targeted at the end of the output, where errors cluster [5.24]. The pre-send checklist checks structure (connectors, language, foundation), not fidelity.
- No anti-sycophancy rule for user-proposed interpretations or for the "note of my own".
- No interactive or step-by-step mode. Delivery is one-shot only, though the evidence favors a narrative default with an opt-in check [5.25][5.28].
- No evidence hygiene for research claims in sard-article/roots/book: no study type, scope, or contested-vs-consensus distinction, and no source-type labels.
- No domain playbooks: domain awareness is a single generic paragraph, with no guidance on what a narrative looks like for code paths, proofs, legal or fiqh reasoning, data analyses or processes.
- Skill engineering: no `references/` folder, no evals or test personas, examples live inline, and the core description lacks Arabic trigger terms and input types.

## Proposals for sard
- P5.1: Add a "scope lock" rule to the core. In the arrival and the "message in brief", keep the author's quantifiers, tense and hedges ("in this study", "some", "may"), and never turn a finding into a recommendation. Any wider generalization goes only in "a note of my own", labeled as inference. Use concrete mechanics, not "be accurate" exhortations, which backfire. (sources: 5.23, 5.24, 5.12) — Fits the narrative core because its "fact → meaning" move is exactly where overgeneralization enters; this keeps the meaning honest without dropping the move.
- P5.2: Add light source anchors. When the source has locators, each station carries one short anchor (slide 7, p. 41, §3, 12:30) at the end of the paragraph or attached to the quote, never as a list of footnotes. (sources: 5.13, 5.20, 5.3) — Prose stays intact while every link in the chain becomes checkable, turning trust into verification.
- P5.3: Add a closing fidelity pass to the pre-send check. Re-read the source against the last two stations, the arrival, the message and the note, and confirm each meaning statement is supported or explicitly labeled as inference. For long sources (books, full lectures), narrate per chunk (chapter or slide group), then write the arrival from the chunk arrivals. (sources: 5.24, 5.32, 5.12) — Protects the arrival, which is the payoff of the narrative and also where errors cluster.
- P5.4: Add "prerequisites → foundation" sizing. Before writing, list the prerequisites the idea depends on, infer from the request and conversation which ones the reader likely has, and put only the missing ones in the foundation. sard-roots handles deeper ones. Ask at most one calibration question, only when the material is long and the level is truly unclear. (sources: 5.26, 5.6, 5.30, 5.8) — The foundation is already a prerequisite step; this makes it explicit and tailored instead of generic.
- P5.5: Add an optional "retell" check after the message in brief: a single line offering 2–3 "why does X lead to Y?" questions built from the narrative's own connectors, or an invitation to retell the path in three sentences, with answers on request. Default it on for study contexts (slides, exams, "I'm studying…") and off for quick explanations. (sources: 5.19, 5.16, 5.30, 5.5) — The connectors are the narrative's logic, so asking the reader to supply them tests whether the narrative was understood rather than merely enjoyed.
- P5.6: Add an interactive narration mode (a flag such as "step by step" or "with me", rather than a new command). Deliver one station at a time and, before each connector, pose one prediction question ("given this, what do you expect the author does next?"). Keep each turn brief and grounded in the source, and let the reader skip to the full narrative at any time. Do not reveal the arrival until the reader has had a turn. (sources: 5.17, 5.18, 5.25, 5.28, 5.27) — Prediction is how stories create suspense, so this is still narration, made active. The opt-in respects the evidence that experienced readers prefer narrative.
- P5.7: Add an anti-sycophancy rule. When the user offers a reading ("so he means X?"), check it against the source and say plainly whether it holds, partly holds or does not. Do not abandon a supported reading because of pushback alone. Write the "note of my own" before considering what the user seems to want. (sources: 5.22, 5.20) — The narrative claims to follow the idea's logic, so it must not bend that logic to please the reader.
- P5.8: Add evidence hygiene for sard-article critique and for external research in sard-roots/sard-book. Name the source type (peer-reviewed, preprint, news, vendor, forum), the study type and scope when a claim rests on research, and whether the point is consensus or contested. Never count papers as votes. (sources: 5.14, 5.38, 5.23) — An honest "however" connector requires knowing how strong the opposing evidence actually is.
- P5.9: Add an optional station map: for long outputs (book, lecture, long article), one compact line or Mermaid chain built from the stations' connectors (foundation → therefore → however → so → arrival), placed after the narrative as a review aid. (sources: 5.11, 5.35, 5.36) — It is derived from the narrative and shows its logical skeleton, which is the thing list-based summaries lack. It never replaces the prose.
- P5.10: Add domain playbooks via progressive disclosure: `sard/references/domains/` with short files (code and systems as the story of a request or data path; math as definitions → claim → why it must be true; fiqh and law as evidence → reasoning → ruling → scope; history; science papers as question → method → finding → limits). Each file gives the domain's natural connectors, its typical foundation, and its fidelity traps. The core loads only the relevant one. (sources: 5.31, 5.32, 5.33, 5.4) — This extends sard to "anyone who needs explanation" without new commands and without bloating the core.
- P5.11: Add evals and personas. Create `evals/` with at least 3 scenarios per command (for example a hedged science abstract, an Arabic slide deck with handwriting, a fiqh passage, an English article for an Arabic reader). Score connector truth, scope fidelity (P5.1), foundation fit for a novice vs an expert persona, and pass rate on the retell check. Run on Haiku, Sonnet and Opus. (sources: 5.32, 5.10, 5.8) — "Connectors must be logically true" is sard's core promise, and evals are the only way to know it holds.
- P5.12: Rewrite the descriptions to Anthropic's spec. Third person, "what + when", with key input types (pdf, pptx, chapter, link) and the Arabic trigger phrases already listed in the body ("ما فهمت", "وضّح لي", "اشرح بالسرد"). Disambiguate siblings so sard-article, sard-book and sard-roots don't compete for the same request. (sources: 5.32, 5.31) — Discovery is the gate. A narrative that never triggers helps no one.
- P5.13: Optionally ship an output style. A plugin `output-styles/sard.md` lets users who want every explanation narrated in a session switch it on. Never use `force-for-plugin`. (sources: 5.4) — It spreads the narrative habit for users who opt in, without hijacking unrelated replies.
- P5.14: Make the closing next step a curiosity hook. Instead of a generic offer, name the open question the next chapter or idea answers. (sources: 5.8, 5.5) — A story's "to be continued" is the narrative version of the curiosity principle, at zero cost.

## Cautions
- **Don't become a withholding tutor by default.** The harm evidence comes from problem-solving practice where answers replace work [5.15][5.17]. sard's typical user needs a text explained, and evidence shows experienced users prefer narrative [5.25]. Withholding by default would frustrate the core audience, and Gemini's experience shows users simply switch modes [5.9]. Keep Socratic opt-in (P5.6).
- **Don't bury the narrative under artifacts.** FAQs, flashcards, quizzes, mind maps and briefing docs are list-shaped [5.11][5.35]. Anything borrowed must be derived from the narrative and placed after it (P5.9) or handed to sard-deck. The main output stays prose with honest connectors.
- **Don't fight hallucination with exhortation.** "Be accurate" prompts doubled overgeneralization [5.23]. Use mechanics instead: scope lock, anchors, an end-of-output fidelity pass, chunk-then-merge. Anchors and grounding reduce errors but don't guarantee correctness [5.12], so keep explicit uncertainty statements.
- **Watch the fluency trap in sard's own success.** A beautiful narrative can leave readers more confident and less capable [5.19][5.16]. Judge success by whether the reader can retell the path, not by how good the explanation feels.
- **Watch for command sprawl.** Each new `/sard-*` command adds trigger competition and maintenance. Prefer modes, flags and `references/` files (P5.6, P5.10) over new commands. Keep the core SKILL.md short (it is about 93 lines now), stay well under 500 lines, and keep references one level deep [5.32].
- **Don't over-ask.** Mollick-style intake asks three sequential questions [5.30], which is too slow for "explain this paragraph". Allow at most one calibration question, and only when it changes the output.
- **Watch for sycophantic notes.** The "note of my own" and article critique are where the model is most tempted to agree with the user [5.22]. Keep the inference tied to the source.
- **Don't self-grade.** Vendor claims of pedagogical superiority are mostly self-evaluated [5.7][5.9]. sard's evals should include rubric-based checks by a separate model run or human spot-checks, plus novice and expert personas [5.10].
